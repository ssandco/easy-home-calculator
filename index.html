Quick answer

For most home projects the math is the same: area = length × width, volume = length × width × depth, then divide by how much one bag, gallon, box or roll covers — and add 10% extra for cuts and waste. Every calculator below does this for you instantly.

Measure once, buy right

Three habits that stop the second trip to the store.

Measure in feet, depth in inches

Length and width are almost always in feet. Depth — mulch, gravel, concrete — is in inches. Every calculator here follows that, so you don't have to convert.

Always add the waste allowance

10% covers cuts, spills and breakage on straight jobs. Go to 15% for diagonal tile, patterned floors, and roofs with valleys.

Round up, never down

Materials come in whole bags, boxes and gallons. Running short mid-job costs more than one extra bag — every result here rounds up for you.

Common questions

Straight answers, in plain words.

How do I calculate how much paint I need?

Measure each wall's width and height, add the areas together, subtract about 21 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window, then divide by 350 — one gallon covers roughly 350 sq ft per coat. Multiply by the number of coats.

How many cubic yards do I need?

Multiply length × width × depth in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27. If depth is in inches, divide the inches by 12 first.

How many bags of concrete do i need?

Work out the volume in cubic feet (length × width × thickness). An 80 lb bag makes about 0.6 cubic feet, a 60 lb bag about 0.45. Divide your volume by the bag yield and round up.

How much extra should I buy for waste?

Add 10% for most projects, 15% for diagonal tile, patterned flooring and complex roofs. It is already included in the defaults — you can change it in each calculator.

What size air conditioner do I need?

Start with about 20 BTU per square foot for a normally insulated room, more for sunny rooms, high ceilings and kitchens. Divide total BTU by 12,000 to get tons.
